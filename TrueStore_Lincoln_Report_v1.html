5 Questions to Ask Before You Sign

The lease is where storage facilities make their money. Here's how to protect yourself.

📅
"What's my rate in month 4?"
The most important question. If the answer differs from what's advertised, that's your red flag. Get the ongoing rate in writing before you sign.
📈
"How often do you raise rates?"
Industry average is 8–10% annually. Facilities with "at our discretion" language in the fine print can raise rates anytime with 30 days notice.
🔒
"Is there a price lock?"
U-Haul offers a 1-year price lock as standard — this is the gold standard. Ask any facility if they'll match it in writing.
💳
"Are there fees beyond rent?"
Watch for: admin/setup fees, mandatory insurance ($15–$20/mo), credit card surcharges (StorageMart charges 2.5%), and lock purchase requirements.
🤝
"Do you price-match?"
Especially powerful at independent facilities without online pricing. Show them a competitor's rate — many will match or beat it rather than lose the rental.

How We Score

TrueStore's transparency score (1–10) measures how clearly a facility discloses real ongoing pricing — not promotional rates — before a consumer commits. We evaluate: whether ongoing rates are published online, whether rate-increase language appears in terms or fine print, whether fees (admin, insurance, credit card surcharges) are disclosed upfront, and whether a price lock or rate guarantee exists.

A score of 8–10 means a consumer can make a fully informed decision from the website alone. A score of 4–6 means pricing exists and is accessible to consumers in a browser, but requires extra steps or inquiry to confirm the ongoing rate. A score of 1–3 means pricing is either hidden behind fine print designed to mislead, or not available online at all.

Facilities are not penalized for using modern web technology that loads pricing dynamically — only for what that pricing reveals (or conceals) once it loads. Data collected June 26, 2026. Prices subject to change — verify before signing.

Note on independent operators: A low score does not automatically mean a bad experience. Small independents with no online pricing may offer better value and more flexibility than chains with polished websites and deceptive fine print. Always call and ask directly.
